01 / THE PROBLEM

Visitors needed a clearer route through the product story.

The web experience needed stronger navigation, more deliberate content hierarchy and responsive layouts. The challenge was to help visitors understand the product and find relevant information across screen sizes.

Starting point

  1. Navigate a less coherent information structure
  2. Work through competing content priorities
  3. Find the relevant product information across layouts

Design direction

  1. Use streamlined navigation to choose a path
  2. Scan a clearer typographic hierarchy
  3. Read related product information in consistent sections
  4. Continue the same journey on smaller screens

02 / RESEARCH & DISCOVERY

The needs behind the interface.

First-time visitors

A clear explanation of the product and where to start.

Visitors comparing capabilities

Readable product information and navigation that helps them find a relevant feature.

Mobile visitors

A usable hierarchy and layout without relying on desktop space.

Research and discovery informed streamlined navigation, clearer typography and a mobile-first structure. The design work connected content decisions with consistent interface patterns across the website.

Research documentation to add

Add the discovery methods, the original information architecture and the specific findings that informed the revised hierarchy. Do not infer improved conversion from the visual redesign alone.

03 / THE DESIGN REASONING

Four decisions that shape the experience.

  1. 01

    Structure information around navigation

    Streamlined navigation and clearer hierarchy help visitors find relevant product information.

  2. 02

    Design for smaller screens

    A mobile-first structure informs responsive layouts rather than treating them as an afterthought.

  3. 03

    Use a consistent visual language

    Shared typography and interface patterns bring separate product sections into a coherent experience.

  4. 04

    Connect content and interface decisions

    The work spans both information structure and visual design. Clearer typography is useful when it supports an understandable sequence of product information.

05 / FROM DESIGN TO EVIDENCE

The next iteration to validate.

The current material explains the approach, but does not document a complete tested iteration. This is a proposed test plan, not a claim that these sessions already happened.

Question

Can a first-time visitor quickly find the information relevant to a specific product need?

Proposed revision

Use task-specific navigation labels and make the relevant section heading easier to identify.

What to observe

Run the same information-finding task on desktop and mobile. Record the first destination, wrong turns, completion and the visitor’s explanation of what they found.

What to measure next.
